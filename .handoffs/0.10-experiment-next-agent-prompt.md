## Step 2: define a comparison that isolates its claimed effect

The conceptual arms are:

A. Full original policy.
B. Source-preserving slice.
C. Semantic specialization of the matched selection.

The central invariant is:

> A B/C comparison cannot isolate transformation if B and C select different policies.

Specify:
- the selection unit: source section, policy node, or another existing unit;
- the selected set shared by the paired arms;
- how dependencies and precedence context are retained;
- how source sections map to authored nodes;
- exactly what differs between the arms.

Preserve the existing `compiler_slice` behavior as a control. If you feed its specialization machinery a different shared selection, label that matched-selection experiment accurately rather than claiming it is the unchanged end-to-end compiler.

A new conservative selector may also be worth evaluating, but its comparison with the current compiler includes selection changes. Keep that separate from the matched-selection transformation contrast.

Do not force a misleading three-arm design merely to keep the table small. If source mapping or selection differences prevent a clean contrast, identify the confound and use or propose the smallest necessary control.

In particular:
- If B retains a whole source section containing more rules than C's selected nodes, acknowledge the extra coverage.
- Do not remove qualifications just to make the apparent selections match.
- Original-source wording versus authored node wording can itself be a transformation. Do not attribute their entire difference to branch specialization.
- Treat results as paired contrasts, not an automatically additive decomposition of “selection loss + specialization loss.”
