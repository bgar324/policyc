## 1. The shortest accurate account

Polaris 0.10 expanded the existing request-state architecture, implemented new policy conditions and artifact contracts, and passed its final offline checks. It remains unfrozen development work. The owner subsequently authorized a WIP checkpoint, as recorded in section 17.

The fresh hidden gate failed. Its scorer matched 333 of 440 labeled assertions and all scored assertions on 93 of 180 cases. The important measurement clarification is that these were **deterministic frontend request-state assertions**, not end-to-end assistant behavior or paid policy-preservation trials.

That failure is enough to reject the scored implementation at its development gate. It is not enough to conclude that a typed intermediate representation is fundamentally wrong, that semantic specialization caused most hidden failures, or that relevance pruning alone already works.

The discussion produced a useful distinction:

> Relevance is not satisfaction. A policy can clearly matter to a request while the compiler cannot safely decide whether its prerequisites have already been satisfied.

The leading proposal is to test source-preserving policy slicing against semantic specialization. Retain a relevant rule's original conditional wording when the compiler cannot justify changing it. This is a hypothesis to compare, not an architecture whose superiority has been demonstrated.

The owner's objection also stands: removing irrelevant policy context was the original idea. Returning to it is not automatically a new discovery. A controlled comparison must show whether the return actually improves preservation.

## 2. What happened in the development iteration

### Starting point and motivation

Compiler 0.9 already had the main architecture that 0.10 extended:

- A frontend reads a request into typed `RequestState`.
- Policy nodes declare branches with conditions over that state.
- Evaluation resolves branches, masks obligations, and handles unavailable tools.
- Artifacts record the state and evaluation decisions.
- The deterministic reader and a persisted model-assisted extractor use the same interface.

The [post-v5 handoff](COMPILER-1.0-HANDOFF-v2.md) records the prior paid held-out-v5 result as 104/137, or 75.91% preservation. Its 33 full-only losses were classified as 15 selector gaps, 9 undeclared conditions, 7 frontend misreads, and 2 stochastic losses. Those are v5 diagnoses, not diagnoses of the new 0.10 hidden set.

The handoff also records that authorization was read correctly on every regressed v5 case. That matters: it would be inaccurate to explain every compiler version's failure as the same authorization-reader defect.

Earlier lessons shaped the protocol. Compiler 0.8's confirmation recognizer failed on fresh phrasings after development against spent examples. In 0.9, a branch developed after exposure to a held-back trace failed on fully specified artifact edits outside its field contracts. These incidents motivated isolated authorship, pre-code family statements, and one-shot scoring for 0.10.

### Authorized work and repository state

The owner initially authorized one narrowly scoped failing-baseline commit, followed by offline implementation left uncommitted. The later WIP-checkpoint authorization is recorded in section 17.

The baseline commit is `55862c5`, `test: freeze compiler 0.10 regression baseline`. It contains the visible and five-case regression slices and the initial test contract. At the end of that initial iteration, the implementation had not been committed or pushed.

The session's last recorded `git status --short --branch` output before this document showed 36 modified files, 2 untracked files, and no staged files. Those two new files were the development audit and `src/extractor/contract.ts`. This synthesis is an additional documentation file.

No Polaris experiment or extractor requests were sent to OpenAI or another paid provider during this iteration. Experiment execution used the fake provider. This records experiment spending, not the coding-assistant sessions' usage. No paid noise-floor measurement, Phase F smoke, Phase G regression run, or held-out-v6 run occurred.

### Implementation scope

The implemented changes included:

1. Five new state observations: `currentInformation`, `deferredWork`, `slideTask`, `externalDisclosure`, and `requestedSlideReorder`.
2. One frontend read shared by policy selection and candidate evaluation.
3. Declarative state triggers and exact artifact-operation scopes.
4. Explicit target and change contracts for spreadsheet, document, slide, and PDF mutations, alongside email and calendar contracts.
5. A narrower `approved_without_scope` branch that distinguishes incomplete fields from absence of a field contract.
6. Concrete PDF, spreadsheet, slide, Gmail, and calendar connector obligations, with separate handling of required-but-unavailable capabilities.
7. Effect-aware masking that distinguishes acting connectors from read-only use when confirmation is required.
8. Source-text safeguards for operation negation, quoted or reported instructions, delegated actors, inline rewrites, ambiguous targets, and slide-order advice.
9. Confidential-disclosure handling with recipient-scoped internal evidence and conservative treatment of unresolved recipient externality.
10. Extractor prompt revision 3, strict persisted-read validation, and a hash covering the prompt, response schema, field inventory, field assignments, and input-envelope matrix.
11. A coordinated cutover to compiler `0.10.0` and artifact protocol `1.3.0`, while retaining historical compiled-artifact compatibility.

The version constants describe the development artifact format. They are not evidence of an accepted compiler freeze.

### Why the work took many passes

Work included design comparison and synthesis, source-only evidence authoring, metadata normalization, label audits, provenance checks, custody-verifier repairs, implementation, and review-driven safety corrections.

The incident ledger records rejected or superseded evidence chains, tool-provenance problems, schema and vocabulary mismatches, and verifier false-pass paths. Some evidence artifacts were reauthored or reaudited before acceptance. A report saying PASS was not always enough to establish valid provenance.

The implementation review returned six initial findings and four targeted re-review findings. Subsequent hardening covered quoted and delegated instructions, attribution across punctuation, misleading target descriptions, mixed negation, and recipient-context mistakes. Passing these regression checks establishes coverage of those controls, not universal safety of the recognizers.

## 4. Important correction: what the hidden scorer actually measured

This clarification emerged while grounding this synthesis in the scorer source. No hidden case data was opened and the scorer was not rerun.

The scorer's `loadWorkingTree` function loads `defaultFrontend` from `src/compiler/evaluate.ts`. That default is `guardedFrontend(deterministicFrontend(), "deterministic")`.

For each hidden request, the scorer obtains one state from the frontend. Its `labelMatches` function compares labeled booleans, enum values, or field maps with that state. The gate score comes from these comparisons.

The source anchors are:

- `src/compiler/evaluate.ts`, lines 9-15.
- `/tmp/policyc-cv010-score/aggregate-scorer/score.mts`, lines 116-141, 155-178, and 304-323.

The reported 333/440 score is therefore a **frontend label-agreement result**. The scored assertions did not measure policy selection, branch effects, masking, emitted instructions, or the answering model's behavior end to end. The case objects' behavioral expectations are not the assertions counted by this scorer.

Consequences for interpreting our conversation:

- "Only half the cases satisfy all their requirements" was too broad. Only half matched all request-state assertions scored for them.
- "The typed representation does not generalize" was too strong. The deterministic implementation failed to produce the expected state labels. The score does not isolate the representational capacity of the schema from the reader's ability to populate it.
- "Semantic specialization caused most of the hidden failures" is unsupported. The scored path did not evaluate specialization.
- "The model-assisted extractor failed the fresh gate" would be false. That extractor was not used for these scores, and revision 3 received no paid extraction evaluation in this iteration.
- The 75.68% figure is not directly comparable to the prior 75.91% paid v5 preservation figure. The denominators and measured events differ.

The blindness safeguard still did useful work. It exposed 107 mismatches between the deterministic frontend and fresh, independently authored state labels, enough to fail the development gate. It did not constitute a full fresh behavioral acceptance test of every new branch, trigger, and mask.

Session chronology records that safety corrections landed after the one-shot score. The scorer evaluated the then-live working tree. The later corrected implementation has no new independent generality score, and a green local suite does not supply that evidence. The recorded percentage applies to the code loaded during the one run, not automatically to every later edit or checkpoint.

There is also a source-identity limitation. The scorer's `repositoryMetadata` records only `HEAD` and whether the tree is dirty. It does not hash the uncommitted compiler files. The custody and scorer hashes therefore do not identify the exact implementation bytes evaluated. The exact scored tree cannot be reconstructed from the scorer record alone. References to a "scored snapshot" in our discussion mean the then-live code, not a verified compiler-source freeze.

## 5. Evidence integrity and its limits

Two datasets must not be conflated.

### The exposed five-case regression slice

`eval/behavioral/compiler-v0.10-regressions-heldback.jsonl` retains its historical filename, but it is no longer held-back evidence.

Its request text was exposed. A temporary diagnostic also emitted a coarse state signature and class information during debugging. One quoted-name-before-noun target form then directly informed a recognizer used to resolve a remaining failure.

The earlier assertion that no exposed wording shaped implementation was incorrect and was corrected in the audit and incident ledger. The final test and assertion messages now call this the **tuned five-case** regression set. The session's captured targeted-test output recorded the title-only cleanup passing, 1/1. The separate 0.9 test label was left unchanged.

Its green result means known regressions are covered. It is not independent generality evidence.

### The fresh five-slice gate

The audit records the fresh gate as request-text and trace blind to the implementation author, with aggregate-only scoring and one-shot use. It is separate from the contaminated five-case slice.

That separation weakens the explanation that the fresh score merely reflects direct fitting to those same hidden examples. It does not establish flawless labels, isolate architectural causes, or erase development overfitting to other exposed examples.

The methodological result is limited but valuable: this frontend failed an independent development check and was stopped. The complete branch-and-behavior claim still lacks fresh end-to-end evidence.

## 6. What `RequestState` assumes

The central abstraction is a structured interpretation of the request that is treated as sufficient for policy decisions.

The intended flow is:

```text
request and context
    -> one frontend RequestState
    -> policy selection and dependency closure
    -> first-true policy branches
    -> obligation masking and conflict checks
    -> unavailable-capability handling
    -> emitted policy prompt
```

Existing selection also uses request text and context. The diagram does not mean every selector decision depends exclusively on state. The key dependency is that semantic branch evaluation and much of the new selection logic trust the shared state.

The answering model still receives the original request. The risk is that the compiler has already removed or changed a governing instruction before that model reads the request.

Typing is useful. It enforces a valid shape, makes contracts explicit, and supports reproducibility. But a valid enum value is not necessarily a correct interpretation.

Consider this invented example, not a hidden fixture:

> "I confirm sending the public report to Dana. Draft the confidential note for the contractor, but do not send that."

Authorization, negation, content, and recipients belong to different proposed actions. A request-wide `authorization=present` or `operationNegated=true` can lose those relationships.

The concern is therefore not just whether the ontology contains enough categories. It is whether facts remain bound to the right actor, action, object, scope, and consequence.

A shared state can remain useful as a transport, cache, or trace format even if it should not be the sole semantic authority for every transformation. Replacing that decision authority would not require declaring all intermediate representations a mistake.

## 7. Relevance, satisfaction, and preserving conditions

The discussion separated three jobs:

- **Selection:** determine which policies matter to this request.
- **Interpretation:** determine what the request actually establishes.
- **Transformation:** use that interpretation to eliminate or specialize a policy instruction.

A policy can be relevant even when its satisfaction is unresolved.

For example:

> "Before forwarding confidential material externally, establish the recipient, content scope, disclosure consequence, and confirmation."

A request to forward confidential material makes the policy relevant. The compiler does not necessarily need to decide whether each prerequisite is satisfied. It can preserve the conditional rule for the answering model.

Astra restated the owner's original relevance-pruning goal through the following contrast. The owner agreed with the contrast but challenged why the original goal was being presented back as a new insight:

> "Polaris may work better as a compiler that removes irrelevant policy context than as a compiler that decides whether nuanced policy requirements have already been satisfied."

The alternative is called residualization here. It means evaluating what is established and leaving the unresolved conditional instruction intact in the output.

Retaining an unresolved rule is not the same as replacing it with an unconditional ask. An extra confirmation question can itself break the requested behavior. Nor is disabling the branch evaluator sufficient if the default node text has already lost the original condition.

The fallback must preserve the source semantics, including relevant qualifications, exceptions, precedence, and dependencies. This is more demanding than retaining a short runtime summary or an obligation token with the right name.

## 8. What the other chat session proposed

The first pasted assessment called 0.10 a "failed generalization attempt" and concluded that "the current 0.10 semantic representation still does not generalize sufficiently." It treated the result as strong evidence that enlarging typed state was not enough, advised against adding more global fields, and recommended no freeze or v6 run. It also argued that the blindness protocol had stopped an unproven version before paid evaluation. Those were the initial interpretation and recommendations. Sections 4 and 9 record why Astra later narrowed the architectural diagnosis.

Its later proposal developed three architectural directions and a common transformation rule.

### Policy-local proof obligations

Instead of first summarizing all requests into a universal ontology, each policy declares what establishes its applicability and satisfaction.

The proposed policy model had four parts:

- `A_p(x)`: when the policy applies.
- `S_p(x)`: what establishes that its requirement is satisfied.
- `E_p`: the effect required otherwise.
- `D_p`: the policies or conditions it depends on.

For a confirmation rule, local questions would cover whether confirmation is needed, what authorization means for this action, whether the request establishes it, whether targets are grounded, and whether disclosure requires separate acknowledgment.

The intended benefit is that a new distinction can live beside its policy instead of requiring another global field and cross-repository changes.

### Proof before elimination

The proposed inversion was to start from the full policy and ask what can be removed, rather than starting empty and predicting what to include.

The rule was:

1. If non-applicability is established, eliminate the node.
2. If satisfaction is established, emit the justified specialized effect.
3. Otherwise, retain the original policy.

The attraction is an asymmetric failure cost. Uncertainty would tend to produce a larger prompt instead of deleting a necessary rule.

This discipline must apply to selection as well as specialization. A cautious branch evaluator cannot recover a policy discarded by an earlier relevance predictor.

### Tiered or hybrid compilation

The proposed tiers were:

- A permanent kernel for general safety and integrity rules.
- Structurally eliminable policy domains where irrelevance can be justified under trusted assumptions.
- Semantically conditional or high-risk policies that retain source wording unless a transformation is established.

Authorization, privacy, destructive actions, current or high-stakes information, and precedence were candidates for the conservative tier.

The other session used an illustrative tradeoff of 85% input reduction with 97% preservation versus 93% reduction with 76% preservation. These were hypothetical design targets, not results of a Polaris experiment.

## 9. Astra's qualifications and revisions

Astra initially accepted too much of the architectural diagnosis. Later replies narrowed it, and the scorer inspection in section 4 narrows it further.

### An LLM verdict is not a formal proof

Changing the question from "is authorization present?" to "does this request satisfy this policy?" can improve context and specificity. It does not make a model's answer sound by definition.

A model returning `entailed`, even with a quotation, is still making a semantic prediction. Its evidence may concern another action, report another person's approval, or omit a qualification.

The term proof would need a defined evidence language, verifier, and assumptions. A high-confidence answer or copied text span does not supply those guarantees by itself.

### Policy-local may still need action-local scope

One policy can apply differently to several actions in a request. The useful unit may be a policy applied to one proposed action, supported by identified evidence, rather than one verdict per policy.

This is a candidate direction, not an implemented action graph or a commitment to build one.

### Shared evidence without a universal verdict

Independent policy queries could duplicate concepts and disagree about the same authorization or recipient. Astra proposed a small shared evidence record containing exact spans, provenance, and links between claims and proposed actions.

The distinction from the current global state would be its role: preserve evidence for local judgments rather than assign one authoritative semantic verdict to the whole request.

The evidence record would not certify truth. Its value and complexity would still need to be tested against a simpler source-retention baseline.

### Local decisions must compose globally

A policy-local permission cannot override another policy's prohibition or confirmation requirement. Existing masks already compose effects across selected nodes.

Any replacement must preserve this global precedence problem. Moving a condition into a local certificate does not remove interactions among policies.

### The uncertainty guarantee is conditional

"Uncertainty increases prompt size rather than semantic error" is a design objective, not an established theorem.

It depends on reliable abstention, correct elimination decisions, and retention of all necessary source context. An overconfident false entailment can still delete a safeguard. A missing exception can still change the meaning of retained text.

Even tool availability is not a sufficient relevance test. If a tool is unavailable, its policy may still require disclosure of that limitation or prohibit simulated use.

Finally, more policy text is not automatically better model behavior. Residualization may reduce compiler-induced errors without eliminating downstream interpretation failures or interference among instructions.

## 10. Why the proposal feels like returning to the beginning

The owner asked why the recommendation sounded like the original research question being repeated. That concern was not dismissed.

The other session offered a coherent progression:

1. Begin with request-conditioned relevance pruning.
2. Extend compilation into resolving relevant conditions.
3. Observe failures in semantic transformations.
4. Separate pruning from specialization and make the latter justify its complexity.

It characterized this as discovering a boundary inside the original thesis, not returning to square one.

Astra accepted the distinction but rejected treating the narrative as a demonstrated result. In particular, the claim that relevance pruning was the half "carrying the value" remains unproven. Strong compression and high offline selection recall do not establish source-preserving behavioral fidelity.

Astra also offered a different explanation for the detour, explicitly as an inference. Specialization may have been introduced partly to repair meaning lost earlier in policy encoding. If conditional source wording became unconditional `ask_confirmation`, the compiler then needed an authorization reader to suppress a question that the original policy did not always require.

Under that hypothesis, the sequence was not merely "specialize to compress more." It was "discard a condition, then build request interpretation machinery to reconstruct when it should apply."

The discussion of 0.7's emitter failures motivates that hypothesis. It does not establish the full historical cause or the cause of the new hidden frontend mismatches.

The agreed intellectual constraint is to avoid retrospective storytelling. Some work may be useful research progress and some may be an avoidable detour. Neither a flattering story nor a discouraging one can replace the missing comparison.

## 11. Efficiency leaves room, but does not settle the design

The recorded structural averages were 16,191 full-policy tokens and 292 compiled tokens. Holding the denominator fixed:

| Illustrative retained prompt size | Approximate reduction |
| --- | --- |
| 584 tokens, twice the current average | 96.4% |
| 1,460 tokens, five times the current average | 91.0% |

These are arithmetic illustrations, not measurements of a source-preserving prototype. They show that a less aggressive transformation policy might still leave substantial compression.

They do not account for a model-assisted reader's input tokens, output tokens, latency, retries, or cache behavior. Policy-local queries could repeat expensive semantic work. Total pipeline cost matters, not just the final prompt's length.

The inference is that maximum compression may be unnecessary. Whether spending more prompt tokens actually recovers preservation is still an experimental question.

## 12. The proposed comparison, not yet authorized or run

The narrowest proposed comparison separates selection from transformation:

| Variant | Prompt sent to the answering model |
| --- | --- |
| Full policy | Original policy prompt |
| Source-preserving slice | Selected source sections with conditions and necessary context intact |
| Specialized slice | The same selected sections, transformed by the compiler |

The two sliced variants must use the same selected set. Otherwise differences in selection and specialization are confounded. Necessary dependency and precedence context must also be accounted for consistently.

The possible outcomes have different interpretations:

- If the source-preserving slice preserves behavior and specialization degrades it, harmful transformation becomes a better-supported diagnosis.
- If both slices fail, selection, source segmentation, missing context, or downstream behavior remain candidates. Returning to relevance pruning has not solved the problem.
- If specialization improves the tradeoff, abandoning it wholesale would be premature.
- If results are close, stochastic variation may dominate. The proposed noise-floor measurement matters before interpreting small differences.

Evaluation of a replacement must examine source-grounded behavior and policy preservation. Requiring it to recreate the old `RequestState` fields would reward similarity to the old architecture rather than success at the original task.

The clarified scorer scope also leaves a separate question open: could the current state interface work materially better with a stronger semantic reader? That has not been isolated by the default-frontend gate. It is an unresolved comparison, not permission to run the paid extractor.

Fresh comparisons would require appropriate isolation, frozen expectations, and the existing authorization discipline. This document does not create new fixtures, reopen spent ones, choose a model, set a budget, or authorize execution.

## 13. Decisions, working recommendations, and non-decisions

### Settled for the current snapshot

- The owner wants this discussion preserved before further architectural work.
- The implementation remains unfrozen development work. The initial authorization covered the failing baseline only; the owner's later instruction explicitly authorizes a WIP checkpoint, not a release.
- The scored deterministic frontend failed its fresh gate. Local tests and fake-provider verification do not overrule it.
- The five-case exposed set is tuned regression evidence only. Its historical filename does not confer blindness.
- The five fresh slices are spent and remain closed to further inspection, diagnosis, and tuning.
- No paid experiment or extractor calls, Phase F, Phase G, or held-out v6 proceed from this discussion. A future paid step requires a keyless costed plan and explicit owner authorization.
- No release or paper claim may present 0.10 as accepted, frozen, or behaviorally validated on fresh end-to-end evidence.
- This document is a discussion snapshot, not a compiler freeze or an instruction to begin a rewrite.

The session's previous iteration checklist recorded 46 of 77 items completed and 31 blocked across measurements, paid development, freeze, held-out v6, and publication. Those blocked phases were not completed by the conversation.

### Working recommendations

- Do not respond to the failed gate by immediately adding another batch of global state fields.
- Distinguish source selection from semantic interpretation and specialization.
- Test source-preserving slicing before committing to an elaborate policy-local proof engine.
- Preserve unresolved conditional semantics rather than equating unknown with an unconditional ask, tool call, or deletion.
- Require justification for removal at the selection stage, not only at branch specialization.
- Treat action-bound evidence and global precedence as design constraints if local semantic claims are pursued.

These are recommendations supported by the discussion. They are not an approved final architecture.

### Explicitly not established

- That Polaris is doomed.
- That a global typed state is inherently incapable of supporting the task.
- That semantic specialization caused most of the fresh hidden mismatches.
- That relevance pruning alone achieves acceptable preservation.
- That source-preserving slicing will outperform the current compiler.
- That policy-local entailment judgments are formal proofs.
- That the model-assisted extractor has failed or succeeded on fresh revision-3 evidence.
- That the final corrected tree scores exactly 75.68% on a fresh gate.
- That the next version must cross 90% or 95%, or even be called 0.11 or 1.0.
- That every detour was necessary research progress.

## 14. Questions left open for the owner's review

1. Is the main failure in populating state, binding facts to actions, representing policy conditions, or composing the resulting effects?
2. How much of the original policy meaning is already lost before specialization, during source segmentation and runtime-instruction authoring?
3. Does an existing experiment provide the clean same-selection, source-preserving baseline described here? The discussion did not establish one.
4. Which exclusions can genuinely rely on trusted structure rather than another semantic relevance prediction?
5. What source qualifications and cross-policy dependencies must travel with a retained section?
6. Would policy-local queries reduce interpretation errors, or duplicate the same errors inconsistently across nodes?
7. What evidence could a verifier check independently, rather than merely trusting the semantic reader's confidence or quotation?
8. Can the compiler abstain without turning uncertainty into unnecessary confirmation or unnecessary tool use?
9. Can a simpler source-retention design preserve most compression after accounting for total inference cost and latency?
10. What behavioral gate would test a replacement without requiring it to imitate the old state representation?

One concrete distinction discussed during development illustrates the unresolved representation question: confidential external disclosure is different from confidential external disclosure whose exact consequence the user has acknowledged. The frozen family statement included that distinction, while the three-value disclosure state and conservative risk cap do not independently encode acknowledgment. This is a design question to revisit from source semantics, not a diagnosis of an unseen failed case.

## 17. Latest update: an experiment before another architecture

After reading the synthesis, the other chat session materially revised its recommendation. The owner asked to include that follow-up and then said, "also if there is any WIP please commit those."

### Separate the hypotheses before adding machinery

The follow-up explicitly says not to jump into a new architecture. It identifies six explanations that the previous discussion had mixed together:

1. The deterministic frontend does not populate the expected state accurately.
2. The state representation cannot express some needed distinctions.
3. Selection is wrong even when supplied with good semantic information.
4. Specialization or masking changes the policy's meaning.
5. Source segmentation or runtime-instruction rewriting loses meaning before specialization.
6. The answering model behaves differently even under a faithful residual policy.

The 75.68% label-agreement result bears directly on the first question. It does not independently resolve the others.

### The proposed first experiment is a source-preserving conservative slicer

The follow-up recommends a deliberately limited baseline before policy-local proofs or a shared-evidence architecture:

- Preserve the 0.10 code as failed development work.
- Retain integrity rules and relevant whole source sections.
- Discard a section only with a stated justification that the request cannot activate it.
- Preserve the retained section's original conditional semantics, qualifications, and needed context.
- Do not resolve authorization into a satisfied branch.
- Do not convert uncertainty into an unconditional `ask_confirmation`.
- Do not turn disclosure-state readings into execution decisions.
- Do not rewrite nuanced conditions merely to shorten the prompt.

This is an experimental baseline, not a newly accepted production architecture. The immediate documentation and checkpoint request does not authorize building or executing it.

The motivating question remains the owner's original one:

> If Polaris only decides what policy context is irrelevant, and refuses to decide whether nuanced relevant conditions are satisfied, how well does it work?

### Keep the ablation controlled

The follow-up proposes A: full policy, B: conservative source-preserving slice, and C: current semantic compiler. Its aim is to distinguish selection loss from transformation loss.

The same-selection requirement from section 12 still applies. If B uses a new conservative selector while C uses the existing selector, their difference includes selection changes. That comparison cannot isolate specialization. A matched-selection B/C contrast is needed in addition to any end-to-end comparison of different selectors.

The follow-up's examples of 95% preservation with 85% token reduction, or 80% preservation, are hypothetical outcomes. A strong B result beside a weaker matched C result would support reducing semantic transformation in that setting. It would not prove that selection is universally correct or that the architecture is settled.

A weak B result would show that removing specialization is insufficient. It would not, on its own, show that specialization was unimportant or not the largest loss source in C. Those claims require the paired comparison, source-fidelity checks, and noise calibration.

### Test the reader separately if state remains useful

Only after the source-preserving comparison, the follow-up proposes a fresh, independently authored reading gate for revision 3's model-assisted extractor, if `RequestState` remains relevant.

The purpose would be to compare reading quality without silently changing the representation or expected labels. The current hidden result does not measure that extractor.

The suggested 98% versus 75% outcomes are also hypothetical. High agreement would reduce one suspected source of error but would not guarantee that all critical action-bound facts were correct. Low agreement could reflect reader limitations, prompt defects, ambiguous labels, or representational mismatch. It would not automatically prove that the distinctions are inherently unknowable.

Any such gate requires fresh isolated evidence and explicit authorization before provider calls. The spent five-slice gate remains closed.

### Only later consider optional semantic specialization

If source-preserving slicing works, the follow-up recommends treating specialization as an optional optimization. It must demonstrate improved compression without unacceptable preservation loss.

Shared action-scoped evidence, policy-local transformation claims, and global precedence remain possible later machinery. They should not precede the simpler experiment merely because they sound more rigorous.

This recommendation is a change in research order: isolate mechanisms first, increase architectural sophistication only when a measured need remains.
