\begin{abstract}
Large system prompts behave like policy programs: safety constraints, privacy rules, tool requirements, artifact procedures, and confirmation protocols, most of which are inactive for any one request. We ask whether a policy $P$ and request $x$ can be compiled into a much smaller prompt $\px$ such that a model answering under $\px$ preserves the critical obligations it satisfies under $P$. We report the full course of that inquiry in \policyc, a research prototype: six deterministic compiler versions, five preregistered held-out studies (280 cases, 1{,}680 model executions), and five small development canaries, all on one pinned GPT-5 mini snapshot, for about \$6.9 of recorded provider spend.

Three results are established. First, efficiency is robust: every compiler cut mean input by 89.7--98.2\% and uncached-equivalent cost by 55--68\%, and most of the reduction on this corpus is deduplication of repeated boilerplate. Second, preservation is bounded: conditional preservation of the full prompt's critical passes ranged from 75.8\% to 86.5\% against a preregistered 95\% gate, and each version fixed the failure classes its development set named and lost to the classes the next fresh set named; fixes fitted to spent cases generalized worst. Third, the gate was set above the reference's own consistency: two samples of the full prompt reproduce each other's passes 90.4\% of the time, which is an empirical baseline, not a ceiling, and every paired result must be read beside it.

The remaining evidence explains the plateau rather than patching it. Retaining the policy's verbatim conditional text does not make the model apply the condition; quoting the request's evidence beside the rule makes the model re-verify it; what moves behavior is a directive. A model that reads the verbatim clauses and writes directives recognized three of four fresh confirmation phrasings that six versions of deterministic reading recognized none of, matched the full prompt's passes, and took no unsafe action where the full prompt took three. It costs about \$0.006 and 20--30 seconds per request, roughly the full prompt's whole answer, because the full prompt is cache-served. Cheaper readers, under two contracts including one that lists the policy's request-dependent conditions so the reader need only compare, kept the rule and lost the request; a worklist resolved by the answering model itself did not substitute. The paper's conclusion is a diagnosis: on this provider the semantic reading that generalizes costs what a reasoning model charges for it, a sequential reader cannot reach latency parity, and the open problem is no longer selecting policy text but paying for its reading.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Production system prompts are growing from short behavioral instructions into heterogeneous policy bundles. A single prompt may state when live research is mandatory, which sources are authoritative, how destructive actions must be confirmed, which personal attributes must not be inferred, what tools may be called, how files must be inspected, and which response format is appropriate. Most user requests activate only a fraction of these rules, yet conventional inference supplies the full prompt on every request.

This creates an appealing systems question: can a request-conditioned compiler activate only the policies relevant to the current request? A successful compiler would reduce input tokens, cost, and latency while making the active contract inspectable. The failure mode is asymmetric. Dropping redundant prose is harmless; dropping one confirmation, privacy, or tool-integrity rule can be severe. Average semantic similarity is therefore an inadequate target, and the question has to be asked behaviorally: does the model, given the compiled prompt, do what it does given the whole one?

Generic prompt compression removes low-information passages or tokens while preserving downstream task accuracy \cite{selectivecontext,llmlingua,longllmlingua,llmlingua2}. Policy compilation has a different objective: it must preserve obligations whose importance is conditional on a specific request, tool surface, artifact, or risk. Related alignment work studies explicit principles and privileged instruction levels \cite{constitutional,instructionhierarchy} but does not test request-conditioned removal of privileged policy text.

We formalize the problem as follows. Let $P$ be a policy program, $x$ a user request plus optional structured context, and $S$ a selector. The compiler emits
\begin{equation}
  \px = \operatorname{closure}(S(P,x)),
\end{equation}
where closure adds every transitive policy dependency. Let $C(P,x,y)$ indicate that answer $y$, produced under policy condition $P$, satisfies all independently specified critical obligations for $x$. The primary target is conditional preservation:
\begin{equation}
  \theta = \Pr\!\left[C(\px,x,Y_{\px})=1 \mid C(P,x,Y_P)=1\right].
  \label{eq:preservation}
\end{equation}
Conditioning on full-policy success is essential: if both conditions fail, the pair says nothing about whether compilation preserved a successful obligation-bearing behavior. The primary efficiency target is
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{input}} = 1 - \frac{\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{input}}(\px,x)]}{\mathbb{E}[T_{\mathrm{input}}(P,x)]}.
\end{equation}

This paper reports the inquiry as it ran, in three phases, and states what each phase establishes and what it does not.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Deterministic compilation (compilers 0.5--0.10, held-out v1--v5).} A typed policy graph, a request-conditioned selector with dependency closure, an emitter, and successive attempts to recognize the request's state: a surface-form predicate, a typed request state read by regexes or by a model-assisted extractor, declared branches, field contracts, and effect-aware masking. Five preregistered held-out studies gave a stable efficiency result and a stable failure to preserve.
  \item \textbf{Measurement (noise floor).} The reference's own self-consistency, computed from grades that already existed, showed the preregistered gate had been set above what a copy of the full prompt would score.
  \item \textbf{Mechanism isolation (source-first arms, canaries 1--5).} Emission that keeps the policy's original bytes, evidence bound beside rules, a model that reads the clauses and writes directives, cheaper readers, a condition index that removes the reader's search, and a single-call worklist. Small, isolated, blind-graded canaries with kill conditions fixed before any output separated what moves behavior from what does not, and priced the one mechanism that generalized.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Prompt compression.}
Selective Context filters prompt content using self-information \cite{selectivecontext}. LLMLingua introduces budget controllers and token-level compression for efficient inference \cite{llmlingua}; LongLLMLingua adapts compression to long-context retrieval and position bias \cite{longllmlingua}; LLMLingua-2 frames task-agnostic compression as token classification distilled from a larger model \cite{llmlingua2}. These methods evaluate answer quality, retrieval, or semantic fidelity. \policyc treats policies as typed program elements and evaluates independent obligations, because infrequent policy text may be critical even when it is semantically peripheral.

\paragraph{Principle-guided behavior and instruction priority.}
Constitutional AI shows how an explicit written constitution can guide critique and preference learning \cite{constitutional}. The Instruction Hierarchy formalizes priority among system, developer, user, and tool-originated instructions \cite{instructionhierarchy}. \policyc trains no model and introduces no hierarchy; it asks which privileged instructions can be omitted for one request without changing critical behavior.

\paragraph{Partial evaluation and program slicing.}
The compiler analogy is closer to partial evaluation than to summarization. A partial evaluator specializes a program on known inputs while preserving its semantics \cite{partialevaluation}; program slicing retains the statements that may affect a criterion. Policy prompts are not conventional programs and language models are stochastic interpreters, but the analogy motivates explicit dependencies, conservative closure, and paired semantic tests. The experiments show where it breaks: flattening a conditional policy into unconditional natural language changes behavior even when the right node was selected, and a residual program the interpreter can read is not the same as one it will execute.

\paragraph{Behavioral evaluation.}
Structural recall and behavioral preservation are distinct. A selector can retrieve every annotated node while the emitted wording weakens, strengthens, or misconditions those rules. \policyc never treats its own selected nodes as ground truth: each case carries independently declared obligations, and primary results come from strategy-blind answer comparison.

\section{Measures}
\label{sec:measures}

\subsection{Paired preservation}

For a paired sample, four determinate critical outcomes are possible: both pass; the full policy passes and the compiled prompt fails (\fullonly); the compiled prompt passes and the full policy fails; or both fail. The estimator for Eq.~\ref{eq:preservation} is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\theta} = \frac{n_{\mathrm{both\ pass}}}{n_{\mathrm{both\ pass}} + n_{\mathrm{full\ only}}}.
\end{equation}
Trial-level Wilson 95\% intervals and exact two-sided McNemar tests are reported as descriptive statistics; the samples of one case are clustered and do not create independent situations. Compiler-only passes are reported and never netted against full-only losses: for policy replacement, a lost baseline success is the quantity of interest.

\subsection{Self-consistency of the reference}
\label{sec:selfconsistency}

The paired estimator conditions on one stochastic sample of the full policy passing. If a second independent sample of the full policy would not pass, some of the apparent loss is sampling variance. With $n$ graded full-policy samples per case, every ordered pair of distinct samples $(i,j)$ contributes to
\begin{equation}
  \rho = \Pr\!\left[F_j \text{ passes} \mid F_i \text{ passes}\right],
  \label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
which is Eq.~\ref{eq:preservation} with the full policy in both slots: the score a byte-identical copy of the reference would obtain. $\rho$ is a baseline, not a ceiling. A different representation can be more stable, or more correct, than the reference and exceed it; nothing in the metric forbids that.

\subsection{Three quantities, reported together}

From Section~\ref{sec:noisefloor} on, every behavioral result reports, from the same samples: source-grounded correctness (pass, unsafe action, and redundant re-ask against a rubric written from the policy alone, before any output existed); preservation of the full policy's passes, paired by sample index; and in-run self-consistency $\rho$. The first asks whether the arm did what the policy says; the second asks whether it did what the reference did; the third says how much the reference agrees with itself. A compiler at 94\% preservation against a reference whose self-consistency is 90\%, while improving source-grounded correctness, is a different result from ``failed to reach 95\%,'' and the reports must be able to say so.

\section{Apparatus}
\label{sec:apparatus}

\subsection{Policy representation, selection, and emission}

The synthetic enterprise-agent prompt (16{,}191 tokens, 21 sections) is represented as 46 manually structured nodes in six YAML packs, validated at load and as a graph (no duplicate identifiers or edges, no cycles, no unknown validators). Given request $x$, artifact metadata, and the tool surface, the deterministic TypeScript compiler detects intents lexically, activates universal policies, matches triggers against request text and context, conservatively retains high-impact safety, privacy, and tool-integrity rules, traverses the transitive \texttt{requires} graph, and orders the result. Closure is exact with respect to declared edges; it establishes dependency completeness, not behavioral completeness. The emitter serializes the selection as compact natural-language instructions. The representation is intentionally explicit: the system compiles a structured graph and does not parse arbitrary prose into policies.

\subsection{Compiler lineage}
\label{sec:lineage}

Table~\ref{tab:lineage} summarizes what each version added. The 0.8 specialization stage evaluates an authored predicate after closure and swaps a node's emitted branch when the predicate holds, failing closed otherwise. Compiler 0.9 replaced it with a compiler in the ordinary sense: a \emph{frontend} reads the request once into a typed request state (authorization present, reported, conditional, or absent; a user-stated limit; the allowed deliverable; a refused purpose; a stated output format; which policy-required fields are stated; whether the operation is named or negated), policy nodes declare branches over that state with a declared precedence table, and a printer emits the first branch decided true. Two frontends exist: regex readers, and an \emph{extractor}, one strict structured-output model call per request at compile time whose output is persisted, hashed, and named in the run manifest. Compiler 0.10 extended the state with five further facts, added artifact field contracts and connector scopes, and made masking effect-aware; it is a development checkpoint, never frozen (Section~\ref{sec:c010}).

\subsection{Source-first arms}
\label{sec:sourcearms}

The third phase added emission paths that keep the policy's original bytes and make no semantic decision. \texttt{source\_preserving\_slice} projects the selector's chosen nodes onto whole source sections. \texttt{source\_clause\_slice} projects onto a hand-audited map of 61 clauses, one per prose paragraph, line-anchored and hash-checked against the pinned source; a clause can be removed only by a trusted structural fact (an artifact type or tool declared exhaustively absent by the case), dependencies travel with it, and the twenty sections' nine identical boilerplate lines are emitted once. \texttt{source\_evidence\_bare} and \texttt{\_apply} add verbatim sentences of the request bound to the clauses' declared evidence roles, with no verdict. \texttt{model\_reader\_slice} appends directives written by a separate model call (Section~\ref{sec:reader}). \texttt{condition\_list\_slice} appends the policy's own request-dependent sentences under one resolve-then-act instruction, with no reader (Section~\ref{sec:v5}). The unchanged end-to-end compiler remains a control arm in every run.

\subsection{Model reader}
\label{sec:reader}

The reader is one structured-output call at compile time. Under \emph{contract 1} it receives the verbatim clause slice, the request, and the declared context, and returns $\{\text{condition}, \text{finding}, \text{directive}\}$ triples for each policy condition it finds that the request bears on. Under \emph{contract 2} it also receives the listed conditions of the retained clauses from a frozen index (Section~\ref{sec:index}), and must return exactly one $\{\text{condition id}, \text{holds} \in \{\text{yes}, \text{no}, \text{undecidable}, \text{not-applicable}\}, \text{quote}, \text{directive}\}$ per listed id; the runtime rejects a reading that does not cover the listed ids exactly once. Under both contracts only directives are rendered, after the slice; conditions, findings, verdicts, and quotes stay in the artifact for audit. An empty reading renders the bare slice, so the arm degrades to source-first retention and never manufactures a directive. Each contract is identified by a hash of its prompt, response schema, clause map, index, and input envelope; readings are persisted and named in the answer run's manifest, so a run's identity fixes what was read.

\subsection{Runtime and provenance}

A Python \texttt{asyncio} runtime executes paired trials with bounded concurrency, hard ceilings on calls, tokens, and dollars, atomic raw-response persistence, resumability without repeated paid calls, and a rebuildable SQLite catalog. A keyless dry run prices every plan's worst case before anything is sent; paid execution requires a run-specific typed confirmation. Trial identifiers bind run, case, sample, strategy, model, provider, and candidate; Python verifies compiled-prompt hashes before execution. Every run directory, reader plan, and the catalog are in the public repository (Section~\ref{sec:repro}).

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

Every case specifies a request, artifact context, independently authored obligations and prohibitions, critical obligation identifiers, refusal and tool expectations, and a rubric. Held-out sets v3--v5 were written by context-isolated authoring agents, each given only a brief and the synthetic prompt, cross-audited, and frozen before the corresponding compiler was opened to them; once inspected, a set became development evidence and was never reused as fresh evidence. Primary behavioral results come from strategy-blind semantic packets graded by isolated agents, with grade hashes locked before answer identifiers were joined to the private strategy map.

The canaries of Sections~\ref{sec:canaries}--\ref{sec:v5} tightened the protocol for small sets: an isolated author who saw only the policy and a brief chose the situations and wrote the rubric (and, in canary 5, per-case expected verdicts for the indexed conditions) before any output existed; the case file, rubric, and labels were hash-locked; kill and pass conditions were written down before the first paid call; a separate isolated grader scored one shuffled packet per trial, with tool calls and their arguments, and its transcript was audited for reads outside its workspace; grades were hash-locked before unblinding. Reader-only stages ran before answer runs, with a gate that decided which readers earned one.

Three regularities hold across the lineage. First, each version fixed the classes its development set named and lost to the classes the next fresh set named: 0.7's largest cluster was fifteen pairs of redundant confirmation on already-confirmed actions, 0.8's specialization stage repaired all six spent cases of that class and matched none of the four fresh phrasings an independent author wrote (``no need to check back'', ``everyone's confirmed'', ``go ahead''), and 0.9's extractor then read those phrasings correctly on every regressed case and lost the study in selection and in conditions its representation did not declare. \section{Study 2: Compiler 0.10 and the Hidden Generality Gate}
\label{sec:c010}

Compiler 0.10 extended the request state with current-information, deferred-work, slide-task, disclosure, and requested-reorder facts; added field contracts for spreadsheet, document, PDF, email, and calendar operations, so that a complete action requires authorization in the user's voice, a named non-negated operation, and every operation-specific target field; added exact connector scopes; made masking effect-aware (ask-side confirmation suppresses acting connectors and keeps reads); and revised the extractor prompt. Offline, it passed its visible regression sets and a tuned five-case slice.

Before any paid study, the deterministic frontend was scored one-shot against five hidden slices authored in isolation: 180 rows, 440 assertions, labels never seen by the frontend's author. It matched 333 of 440 assertions (75.68\%) and 93 of 180 rows fully (51.67\%). These are frontend label-agreement scores, not preservation, and they are not comparable to Table~\ref{tab:preservation}; they are also a failed gate. The slices were spent on scoring and never diagnosed. No paid run was made from 0.10, it was checkpointed rather than frozen, and the inquiry changed course: instead of another architecture, isolate the mechanisms that were losing.

\section{Study 3: The Reference's Self-Consistency}
\label{sec:noisefloor}

Equation~\ref{eq:rho} was computed from grades that already existed. On held-out v5, with three blind-graded full-policy samples on 57 of 60 cases (177 ordered pairs):
\begin{equation*}
  \rho = 0.904, \qquad \text{pairwise agreement } 0.851, \qquad 13 \text{ of } 60 \text{ cases split}, \qquad \text{full pass rate } 0.774.
\end{equation*}
On canary 2 (8 cases, 2 samples, a source-grounded rubric) $\rho = 1.000$; on canary 1 (4 cases) $\rho = 0.800$.

A byte-identical copy of the full prompt would have scored 90.4\% on v5's preservation metric and failed the preregistered 95\% gate. Compiler 0.9's 75.91\% is 14.5 points below the reference's self-consistency, not 19 below the gate; that baseline, not the gate, is the honest comparison. The 33 lost pairs of v5 were binned by reading each pair, and the split-case list was not used to re-bin anything. From this point every paired result in the project reports in-run $\rho$ beside it.

\section{Study 4: Source-First Emission, Offline}
\label{sec:sourcefirst}

The 0.10 failure analysis had argued that transformation loss (authored wording, branch resolution, masking) and selection loss should be separated, which requires an arm that keeps the source's own words. Two offline measurements on the 97 visible development cases established what such an arm can and cannot do.

\paragraph{Retention is mostly deduplication.} Whole-section retention of the selector's chosen nodes leaves a 46.59\% mean token reduction. The clause map leaves one prompt of 3{,}259 tokens, identical for every visible case, 79.87\% below the full policy. Nothing was pruned in any case: no visible case declares its context exhaustive, so no structural fact establishes irrelevance under the rules adopted, and every clause is retained. The entire reduction is the audited deduplication of boilerplate: of the source's 16{,}123 section tokens, 13{,}371 are nine bullet lines repeated in twenty sections and 2{,}449 are substantive prose. A real policy that does not repeat itself would gain almost nothing from this approach; the 90\%+ reductions of Table~\ref{tab:efficiency} were partly this corpus's redundancy. With an exhaustive declaration, pruning tops out around 10 of 61 clauses.

\paragraph{Authoring is where conditions die.} Tracing five cases through the compiler stage by stage found that in every one at least one authored YAML default had turned a conditional source sentence into an unconditional instruction before any branch ran: confirmation ``when appropriate'' became always ask; ``notes only when they help'' became a mandatory format; ``inspect before editing'' became a mandatory tool call; ``prefer drafts'' became ``do not draft''. Only 18 of 97 visible cases take a branch at all; 79 ship those defaults.

\section{Study 5: Canaries 1--3, from Retention to a Reader}
\label{sec:canaries}

Three small paid canaries then tested, in order, whether the answering model applies a preserved condition unaided, whether quoting the request's evidence beside the rule helps, and whether a model that reads the clauses and writes directives does what the deterministic reader could not. Each used fresh cases from an isolated author, a rubric locked before outputs, and kill conditions fixed in advance; canaries 2 and 3 used an isolated blind grader. Cases from every canary are spent.

\subsection{Canary 1: a preserved condition is not an applied one}

Four cases, three arms (full, clause slice, current compiler), two samples, \$0.06. Passes of 8: full 5, clause 4, compiler 6. On the one case that tests the hypothesis, a send the user had reviewed word for word (``I have read the final wording below and it is exactly what I want \ldots{} Send it now''), the clause slice re-asked in both samples with the verbatim confirmation rule in its prompt, and so did the compiler, whose regex frontend read \texttt{authorization: absent}. The full policy sent in both samples. The only arm that acted on turn one anywhere in the canary was the compiler, on a case where it emitted a directive; the same reader that produced that directive forwarded privileged material on a misread. Edge and danger were one mechanism.

\subsection{Canary 2: evidence beside the rule makes it worse}

Eight cases, five arms (adding the two evidence-binding frames), two samples, \$0.16. Passes of 16: full 6, clause 5, evidence-bare 5, evidence-apply 5, compiler 4. On satisfied cases the evidence arms re-asked on 4 of 6 samples against the full policy's 2 of 6, and re-asked \emph{about the items the evidence block quoted}: the user's statement that attendees had confirmed became a checklist for the user to confirm again. A confirmed archive was re-asked 10 of 10 times across all arms; no policy-text prompt archived on turn one. The apply frame produced one soft unsafe action. Both kill conditions were met and evidence binding was abandoned. The lesson, carried into every later design: the model re-verifies quoted facts and follows instructions; what moves behavior is a directive.

\section{Study 7: Condition-Indexed Reading and a Single-Call Worklist}
\label{sec:v5}

\subsection{Removing the search}
\label{sec:index}

Canary 4's readers appeared to fail at a search: asked to find the request-dependent rules in the slice, they emitted the salient rule instead of comparing the request to it. Contract 2 does the search once, offline. A source-first \emph{condition index} was audited over the entire policy under one criterion: a condition is a sentence whose directive for this turn depends on what the request itself states (whether the user has confirmed, authorized, requested, or limited something, or left something unstated). Every one of the 61 clauses was classified; triggers (which name when a duty applies), definitions, conditions on facts the request cannot settle (tool failure, artifact contents), compiler meta-instructions, and restatements were excluded. Sixteen conditions over twelve clauses survived, each a verbatim sentence and the verbatim span the request must meet, tested as substrings of the pinned source. Six of the sixteen belong to classes no canary had exercised; the index was frozen, and the owner forbade adding, removing, paraphrasing, or tuning any entry on subsequent evidence.

The retained clauses' indexed conditions are listed for the reader, and the reader answers each exactly once (Section~\ref{sec:reader}). Because the clause map retains conservatively, a request lists 12--14 conditions, of which about eight are inert for it; that cost was measured rather than trimmed. Alongside, \texttt{condition\_list\_slice} appends the same sentences to the answer prompt under one instruction to resolve them from the request's words before acting, so that the two arms separate the question ``is a finite worklist enough?'' from ``does a separate reader matter?''

\section{Synthesis}
\label{sec:synthesis}

\subsection{What the evidence establishes}

\paragraph{Efficiency is robust and partly an artifact of redundancy.} Five frozen studies and every canary produced one to two orders of magnitude less input and at least 55\% lower uncached-equivalent cost. On this corpus, 83\% of the source's tokens are repeated boilerplate, and an audited deduplication alone reaches 80\%; genuine per-request pruning under conservative rules reaches about 10 of 61 clauses. Prompt caching then erodes the bill: a full prompt served 71--78\% from cache is cheap per call, and the compiled arm's saving was a third, not an order of magnitude.

\paragraph{Preservation is bounded, and the bound has been explained rather than patched.} Deterministic compilation preserved 75.8--86.5\% of the reference's critical passes on five fresh sets against a reference that reproduces itself 90.4\% of the time. The losses had addresses in every version, the addresses moved with each fresh set, and the fixes fitted to spent cases generalized worst. Three canaries then located the mechanism: the model acts on directives, not on preserved conditions or quoted evidence; and the directive that generalizes to fresh phrasing comes from a model reading the source clauses, not from any enumeration of how people say ``I approve.''

\paragraph{The semantic reading that generalizes costs what a reasoning model charges for it.} The one reader that read fresh confirmations correctly and took no unsafe action spends \$0.006--0.008 and 20--30 seconds per request; on this provider that is the whole full-policy answer, and under a contract that lists the conditions it costs more, not less. Three cheaper configurations under two contracts kept the rule and lost the request, and one of them read the privileged disclosure as authorized. A sequential reader cannot reach latency parity in this design: the reader arm's own answer leaves under half a second for the read.

\paragraph{The reference is not an oracle.} The full prompt took three unsafe actions in canary 3 and two in canary 5, and re-asks a third of the time on confirmed actions in canary 2. Preservation measures agreement with a reference that is itself only 90\% self-consistent and sometimes wrong in the dangerous direction; source-grounded correctness must be reported beside it, and an arm can legitimately be safer than the reference while preserving less of it.

\subsection{What it does not establish}

No preservation rate for the reader architecture: canary 3 is eight cases with an in-run $\rho$ of 2 of 6, and the direction is consistent with the hypothesis while the magnitude is unmeasured. Whether the reference reader's directives under contract 2 change downstream behavior: the answer run with its readings was designed and not run. Anything about other providers or model families, where cached-input pricing or reasoning cost may invert the economics either way. Anything about real organizational prompts, which do not repeat themselves twenty times.

\subsection{The economics, stated}

Three quantities describe the bill and must be kept apart: actual billed cost, generation-only cost, and uncached-equivalent cost. The first is what the user pays and is dominated by caching; the last isolates the compiler's context effect. A compiled prompt's saving is bounded by the cache discount on the prompt it replaces, and any per-request semantic step is charged at fresh-output prices. On this provider the full policy's answer costs about \$0.003--0.005; a reader that fits under it must produce its reading in a few hundred output tokens with no reasoning, and no such reader read.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{Structured input.} The compiler begins from manually structured YAML nodes and a hand-audited clause map and condition index. Automatic extraction of policies, dependencies, and conditions from arbitrary prose remains unsolved.

\paragraph{Single model and synthetic environment.} All paid studies use one pinned GPT-5 mini snapshot, its nano sibling as a reader candidate, and a synthetic enterprise policy whose redundancy inflates compression. Results may differ across models, providers, and real prompts.

\paragraph{Changing held-out sets and small canaries.} Each compiler was evaluated on a newly authored set, which protects holdout discipline and prevents controlled cross-version comparison. The canaries have four to eight cases and two samples; they separate mechanisms and price them, and they do not estimate rates.

\paragraph{Clustered samples.} Three (or two) generations per case measure model variability, not independent situations. Intervals and tests are descriptive.

\paragraph{Agents as authors and graders.} Cases, rubrics, labels, and grades were produced by isolated language-model agents, not human experts; isolation was audited from transcripts. The canary 3 reader prompt and the canary 3 situations were chosen by the same agent that ran the study; canaries 4 and 5 removed that link by letting the author choose situations within condition classes. Human inter-annotator reliability is not claimed.

\paragraph{Harness.} Tool calls are recorded, not executed, so a turn whose first call is a lookup ends; canary 5 lost three confirmed cases to identifier parameters that invited a lookup. The context contract was asymmetric in one v3 pair. Both are documented and neither was corrected after unblinding.

\paragraph{Execution amendments.} Provider-adapter and accounting fixes occurred during v0.6 and v0.7 execution and an input-reservation fix before v4; they were narrowly scoped, documented before unblinding, and did not touch cases, prompts, obligations, or parameters.

\paragraph{Spent evidence.} Every held-out set, hidden slice, and canary set is spent; the compiler-0.10 five-case ``held-back'' slice was exposed during development and is recorded as tuned. Nothing in this paper reuses a spent set as fresh evidence.

\section{Conclusion}

\policyc set out to compile a policy prompt into a request-specific slice that preserves the model's critical behavior. It established that most policy text is inactive for most requests and that a compiled prompt reliably removes it, and that on a redundant corpus much of the removal is deduplication. It established, on five preregistered sets, that deterministic compilation preserves about three quarters to six sevenths of the reference's critical passes and that the reference reproduces itself nine times in ten, so that the remaining gap is real but smaller than the gate implied. It then established what the gap is made of: not selection, and not compression, but reading. The model acts on directives; the directive that generalizes to how people actually write comes from a model reading the policy's own sentences; that reading costs, on this provider, about what the whole full-policy answer costs, and every attempt to buy it cheaper, with less reasoning, a smaller model, or a contract that removed the search, produced a reader that kept the rule and lost the request. A worklist the answering model resolves for itself did not substitute.

The research outcome is a bound with a mechanism attached. Request-conditioned compilation of policy prompts is limited by the price of reading the request, not by the difficulty of slicing the policy; and reading the request is a task the cheap tiers of this provider do not perform under either contract tried. Whether that price falls with better models, better caching of readings across repeated requests, or a fast deterministic path with semantic fallback on the ambiguous fraction of traffic is the question this work leaves open, with the instruments to answer it: frozen sets, locked rubrics, a condition index over the source, a contract that makes a reading a checkable verdict, and a gate that decides before money is spent.
